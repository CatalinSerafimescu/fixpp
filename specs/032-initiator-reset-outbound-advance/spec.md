# Feature Specification: Initiator reset_on_logon Outbound Seqnum Restore on Peer 141=Y Echo

**Feature Branch**: `032-initiator-reset-outbound-advance`
**Created**: 2026-06-11
**Status**: Draft
**Input**: User description: "Fix the live-found L-024-2 conformance bug: a fixpp INITIATOR with reset_on_logon=true emits Logon(141=Y, 34=1) (outbound advances to 2), then on the peer's 141=Y Logon-ack ECHO it rebases its OUTBOUND seqnum 2→1, so the next outbound frame duplicates 34=1 and both QuickFIX-cpp and QuickFIX-J reject it. The peer's echo of fixpp's OWN reset must not trigger a second outbound reset. Missing OUTBOUND twin of the 030 fix (which restored only the INBOUND counter on this same arm). Must NOT regress the peer-spontaneous reset case. Do NOT anchor on 'symmetric restore' — 030's first prototype was a half-fix."

## Overview

This is a **conformance bug fix** for a live-found defect in the merged `024-reset-refresh-on-logon` feature (catalogue row **S-017**, the `ResetOn{Logon,Logout,Disconnect}` knobs), tracked as limitation **L-024-2** in `spec/behaviors-and-limitations.md`. It is invisible to the in-process unit tests as originally pinned and was caught only on the **first live run** of the `RL-{QFcpp,QFj}-init-fix44-reset-on-logon` interop cells — exactly paralleling the `030` (received-141 acceptor inbound) and `031` (acceptor 789 boundary) off-by-ones that live interop uniquely surfaced. It is the **missing OUTBOUND twin** of the `030` fix.

When fixpp is an **initiator** with `reset_on_logon=true`, it resets its sequence numbers before logging on and emits `Logon(141=Y)` at sequence number `1`; sending that Logon consumes sequence `1`, so its next outbound becomes `2`. A conformant peer acceptor confirms the reset by **echoing** `141=Y` in its Logon-ack. fixpp honors that echoed reset on the initiator `peer_ack_sent_reset_flag` arm: it calls the shared reset helper, which rewinds **both** counters back to `1`. The `030` fix added a restore of the **inbound** counter on this arm (the peer's consumed seq-1 reset-ack Logon is a surviving net-advance → next-inbound restored to `2`), but it left the **outbound** counter rewound to `1`. Because fixpp's own reset Logon already consumed sequence `1`, the next outbound frame is then emitted at `34=1` again — a **duplicate sequence number**. Both QuickFIX-cpp v1.16.0 and QuickFIX-J 3.0.1 reject the duplicate ("MsgSeqNum too low") and the session fails to stay established.

The correct behavior — matching the reference engines' reset-then-increment parity and fixpp's own already-correct inbound restore — is: after honoring the peer's echo of fixpp's **own deliberate** reset, fixpp's next-outbound must remain `2` (the reset Logon consumed seq `1`), never rebased to `1`. The peer's echo of a reset fixpp itself initiated must not trigger a *second*, spurious outbound reset.

**Design trap (recorded, not to be repeated):** the `030` prototype was initially a half-fix. The outbound restore is **not** a blind mirror of the inbound restore, and the suppression trigger is **not** "fixpp initiated the reset" in the policy sense (`bilateral_strict` / `we_initiated`). The inbound restore is conditioned on the *peer's* reset-ack Logon having been consumed; the correct outbound condition is narrower: fixpp **reset its counter before sending its Logon, so that Logon went at the post-reset sequence `1` and consumed it**. That is any of the knob-driven reset-before-send paths — `reset_on_logon` (live-proven) and, structurally on a reconnect, `reset_on_logout` / `reset_on_disconnect`. It must be distinguished from three other paths that must stay unchanged: (i) the peer **spontaneously initiates** a reset fixpp did not request (`reset_on_logon=false`, fixpp sent no `141=Y`); (ii) fixpp requested a reset under `bilateral_strict` but its Logon was emitted at a **pre-reset** sequence `N > 1` (the counter was not reset before send), in which case the post-reset next-outbound of `1` is **correct** because no post-reset frame consumed sequence `1`; (iii) the acceptor role. Anchoring the design on "symmetric restore to seqnum_min+1" or on `we_initiated` without the reset-before-send condition would either mis-handle the peer-spontaneous path or wrongly rebase the pre-reset-Logon case.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-06-11

- Q: The outbound-rebase bug fires when fixpp's own reset Logon went at the post-reset seq `1` and the peer echoes `141=Y`. The live finding proved the `reset_on_logon` path; `reset_on_logout` / `reset_on_disconnect` can structurally reach the same arm on a reconnect (they also emit `141=Y` per the `session.cpp:716` OR-predicate and can produce a seq-1 Logon). Which sessions should the fix cover? → A: **All reset-before-send paths.** Key the fix on the structural condition — fixpp emitted `141=Y` in its own Logon **and** that Logon went at the post-reset seq `1` — which naturally covers `reset_on_logon`, `reset_on_logout`, and `reset_on_disconnect`. `bilateral_strict` alone (which emits `141=Y` but does **not** reset the counter before send, so its Logon may go at seq `N > 1`) is correctly excluded by the seq-1 condition. Only `reset_on_logon` is live-proven; the other two are structurally reasoned and covered by construction.
- Q: The bug-case session (`reset_on_logon=true` + `bilateral_lenient`) currently emits `sequence_numbers_reset{by_peer_request=true}` even though fixpp initiated the reset (`we_initiated = (policy == bilateral_strict)` is the only initiated-path today). Should the fix also correct this event label? → A: **Also fix the label.** The fix sets `by_peer_request=false` whenever fixpp itself emitted `141=Y` in its own Logon (via any of `bilateral_strict`, `reset_on_logon`, `reset_on_logout`, or `reset_on_disconnect`). The event label (`by_peer_request`) keys off the **latched emit-time fact** `own_logon_sent_reset_flag_` ALONE, while the outbound restore additionally requires the seq-1 observable (`reset_before_send`) — i.e. two DISTINCT gates that diverge on the `bilateral_strict`-at-N row (latch true → `by_peer_request=false`, but the Logon went at seq N>1 so restore does NOT fire).

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - reset_on_logon initiator establishes and stays up against a conformant peer (Priority: P1)

An operator runs fixpp as a FIX **initiator** with `reset_on_logon=true`, interoperating with a counterparty acceptor. fixpp resets and logs on with `Logon(141=Y)` at sequence `1`; the peer confirms the reset by echoing `141=Y` in its Logon-ack. fixpp must reach the established state and then send its **next** outbound message at sequence `2` (not a duplicate `1`), so the peer accepts subsequent traffic and the session stays up.

**Why this priority**: This is the headline `reset_on_logon` initiator case against any conformant counterparty. Today fixpp rebases its outbound to `1` after the peer's echo, so its very next send duplicates the Logon's `34=1`, the peer rejects with a "MsgSeqNum too low" Logout, and the session cannot carry traffic. The `reset_on_logon` knob is therefore effectively broken for fixpp-as-initiator against real engines. Fixing this restores the headline `024` capability on the initiator side and is the precondition for the `RL-*-init` live interop cells to pass.

**Independent Test**: Drive an initiator session with `reset_on_logon=true`. After `open()` emits `Logon(141=Y)` at seq `1` (next-outbound advances to `2`), feed a peer Logon-ack that echoes `141=Y` at seq `1`. Assert: (a) the session reaches the established (Active) state; (b) fixpp's next-outbound is `2` (not `1`); (c) the next frame fixpp emits carries sequence `2`, not a duplicate `1`. Verifiable in-process by inspecting the seqnum counters and emitted frames (the RED harm witness `DISABLED_ResetOnLogon_Initiator_PeerAck141_OutboundStaysTwo` already pins exactly this), and live by the `RL-*-init` interop cells against QuickFIX-cpp / QuickFIX-J.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a fixpp initiator with `reset_on_logon=true` whose reset Logon was emitted at seq `1` (next-outbound at `2`), **When** the peer's Logon-ack echoes `141=Y` at seq `1`, **Then** fixpp reaches the established state and keeps its next-outbound at `2`.
2. **Given** the same session, **When** fixpp emits its next outbound message, **Then** that message carries sequence `2` — no emitted frame re-uses the sequence number already consumed by the reset Logon.
3. **Given** the same session, **When** the inbound counter is inspected, **Then** next-inbound is `2` (the `030` inbound restore is preserved — proving the fix reached the right arm and only adds the missing outbound twin).

---

### User Story 2 - Peer-spontaneous reset is unaffected (non-regression) (Priority: P1)

An operator runs a fixpp initiator that did **not** deliberately reset (it did not send `141=Y`; `reset_on_logon=false`) under a reset-seqnum policy that tolerates a peer-initiated reset. The peer's Logon carries `141=Y` spontaneously. The fix must change **only** the case where the peer echoes fixpp's own deliberate reset; the observable outbound behavior of the peer-spontaneous (peer-initiated) reset path must be exactly as shipped today, and must match the reference engines.

**Why this priority**: The fix narrows when the outbound rebase is suppressed. It must suppress it **only** when the reset originated from fixpp itself. If the suppression were applied indiscriminately, a genuine peer-initiated reset would no longer rebase fixpp's outbound and would mis-number subsequent traffic the opposite way. Preserving the peer-initiated path is as load-bearing as fixing the echo path — this is the explicit design axis the `030` half-fix lesson warns about.

**Independent Test**: Drive an initiator session with `reset_on_logon=false` under a tolerant reset-seqnum policy, fixpp's outbound pre-advanced so its Logon went at seq `N > 1` with **no** `141=Y` from fixpp, then feed a peer Logon carrying `141=Y`. Assert the post-reset outbound counter equals the value pinned against the reference engines in `research.md` / `plan.md` and is **unchanged** from the shipped baseline for that path; the `sequence_numbers_reset` event's `by_peer_request` classification is unchanged.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a fixpp initiator with `reset_on_logon=false` that did not send `141=Y` and whose Logon went at seq `N > 1`, **When** the peer spontaneously sends a Logon with `141=Y` under a tolerant policy, **Then** fixpp's post-reset outbound counter is exactly what it is today (the reference-engine-pinned value), and the `sequence_numbers_reset{by_peer_request=true}` event classification is unchanged.
2. **Given** the `reset_on_logon=true` echo path of User Story 1 versus the peer-spontaneous path above, **When** both complete, **Then** they are observably distinguished: the echo path keeps outbound at `2` (the reset Logon's consumed seq), the spontaneous path follows the unchanged peer-initiated semantics.

---

### Edge Cases

- **Peer omits 141=Y under bilateral_strict**: when fixpp (initiator, bilateral_strict) sent `141=Y` and the peer's Logon-ack omits it, fixpp must continue to reject with `session_seqnum_reset_mismatch(116)` and disconnect — the existing arm, unchanged.
- **Peer's echo Logon-ack at a sequence number other than 1**: out of scope for the boundary change; the inbound handling of the peer's reset-ack sequence is owned by `030`. This feature changes only fixpp's **outbound** counter after the reset is honored.
- **Acceptor role**: unchanged. The acceptor received-141 inbound/outbound semantics are owned by `030`; the acceptor's outbound reply already stays correct (independent counter). This fix touches only the **initiator** `peer_ack_sent_reset_flag` arm.
- **Knob disabled / no reset on either side**: with neither side resetting, the `peer_ack_sent_reset_flag` arm is not entered and behavior is byte-identical to the pre-fix baseline.
- **Persistent store**: with a persistent message store, the corrected outbound value must be durably consistent (no `store > manager` over-persist and no `store < manager` under-persist that would skip or duplicate on restart), preserving the `029` INV-H1 lower-bound invariant on this arm.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: When fixpp is an **initiator** that deliberately reset its sequence numbers via any of its reset knobs (`reset_on_logon`, `reset_on_logout`, or `reset_on_disconnect`) — so it emitted its Logon with `141=Y` at the post-reset sequence `1` and its next-outbound advanced to `2` — and the peer's Logon-ack **echoes** `141=Y`, the system MUST keep its next-outbound at `2` after honoring the echoed reset. It MUST NOT rebase outbound to `1` — the reset Logon already consumed sequence `1`. (`reset_on_logon` is the live-proven case; the other two reach the same arm structurally on a reconnect and are covered by the same seq-1 condition.)
- **FR-002**: On the path of FR-001 the session MUST reach the established (Active) state, exactly as it does today (the fix changes the outbound counter, not the state transition).
- **FR-003**: On the path of FR-001 the system MUST NOT emit any subsequent frame whose sequence number equals the sequence number already consumed by fixpp's reset Logon. The first message fixpp originates after the established transition MUST carry sequence `2`.
- **FR-004**: The system MUST preserve the `030` inbound restore on this same arm — after the peer's `141=Y` echo, next-inbound MUST be `2` (the peer's consumed seq-1 reset-ack Logon is a surviving net-advance). This fix only **adds** the outbound twin; it must not alter the inbound restore.
- **FR-005**: The fix MUST suppress the outbound rebase **only** when BOTH conjuncts of FR-001 hold: (a) fixpp actually emitted `141=Y` in its own Logon on this connection (the captured emit-time fact, gating FR-006), AND (b) that Logon went at the post-reset sequence `1` (next-outbound was `2` at arm entry). It MUST NOT change the observable outbound behavior of any of: (i) the **peer-spontaneously-initiates** path (fixpp sent no `141=Y`, so conjunct (a) is false — including a fresh non-resetting initiator whose own `Logon(34=1)` predates a peer-declared reset epoch, where outbound MUST stay `1`); (ii) the path where fixpp requested a reset (`bilateral_strict`) but its Logon was emitted at a **pre-reset** sequence `N > 1`, so conjunct (b) is false (post-reset next-outbound of `1` is correct there); (iii) the acceptor role. For each of these the post-reset outbound value MUST be unchanged from the shipped baseline and MUST match the reference engines; values are pinned against QuickFIX-cpp / QuickFIX-J in `research.md` / `plan.md`.
- **FR-006**: The `sequence_numbers_reset` event emitted on this arm MUST classify `by_peer_request=false` **iff fixpp actually emitted `141=Y` in its own Logon on this connection** — the literal emit-time fact, not an enumeration of which knob/policy was set — and `by_peer_request=true` only when the peer initiated a reset fixpp did not request. The emit-time fact is the `141=Y`-emit predicate already computed when the Logon is built (`session.cpp:721` `initr_reset_seqnum`); the fix MUST capture that exact value at emit time and read it here, rather than reconstruct it on the ack arm from config flags plus a counter sample (a reconstruction that drops the inbound-at-1 conjunct of the real emit predicate and would mislabel a hydrated initiator). This corrects the present classification (`we_initiated = (policy == bilateral_strict)`), which mislabels the fixpp-initiated `reset_on_logon` + non-strict-policy path as `by_peer_request=true`. The counter correction (FR-001) and this event classification MUST both gate on the **same** captured emit-time fact ("fixpp emitted `141=Y`"); the counter correction additionally requires fixpp's Logon to have gone at the post-reset seq `1` (FR-005).
- **FR-007**: With a persistent message store, the corrected outbound counter MUST be durably consistent on this arm — preserving the `029` INV-H1 store-vs-manager lower-bound (no over-persist, no under-persist that would mis-number on restart). The fault disposition (reset failure under a persistent store) MUST follow the existing `030` fatal-when-persistent contract.
- **FR-008**: The fix MUST NOT change the observable behavior of the **acceptor** role, the **knob-off** default path, or any path that does not enter the initiator `peer_ack_sent_reset_flag` arm — these MUST be byte-identical to the pre-fix baseline.
- **FR-009**: The fix MUST introduce **no** new wire field, error code/slot, generated-code change, configuration surface, or C-ABI surface; it is a pure correction of an existing internal sequence-counter computation.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: With `reset_on_logon=true` on a fixpp initiator whose reset Logon went at seq `1` (next-outbound `2`), after the peer's `141=Y` Logon-ack echo the session reaches the established state and next-outbound is `2` — verified by an automated test (the existing RED harm witness `DISABLED_ResetOnLogon_Initiator_PeerAck141_OutboundStaysTwo` flips to enabled and green for the Active + `peek_outbound()==2` + inbound==2 assertions), and live by the `RL-{QFcpp,QFj}-init-fix44-reset-on-logon` interop cells against running QuickFIX-cpp / QuickFIX-J. (The wire-`34=2` originate-and-capture is owned by SC-002, not this counter assertion.)
- **SC-002**: On the SC-001 path, the next message fixpp originates carries sequence `2` and no emitted frame re-uses the reset Logon's sequence `1` — verified by an automated test that, after the established transition, **originates a post-Active outbound frame through the unit harness, captures the emitted bytes, and asserts wire `34=2` with no second frame at `34=1`**. The committed counter-only harm witness (`DISABLED_..._OutboundStaysTwo`, which asserts `peek_outbound()==2`) is a supporting check, not the wire proxy — SC-002 requires the originated-frame `34=2` assertion (the counter could be set for the assertion while send/store ordering still regressed at the next emit, [[feedback_witness_asserts_named_postcondition_not_proxy]]). W1 is EXTENDED (or a paired sub-witness added) to carry this originate-and-capture. Today this is violated (the next send duplicates `34=1`).
- **SC-003**: The live `RL-*-init` interop cells establish and the counterparty does **not** reject with a "MsgSeqNum too low" Logout — verified by the live interop harness wire transcript; the two cells flip from `deferred:initiator-141echo-outbound-rebase` to passing.
- **SC-004**: The peer-spontaneous (peer-initiated) reset path's observable outbound counter is unchanged from the shipped baseline and matches the reference engines — verified by an automated test asserting the reference-pinned value for the `reset_on_logon=false` + peer-`141=Y` path.
- **SC-005**: 100% of existing session / `024` / `029` / `030` / reset-seqnum-policy regression witnesses remain green after any pins genuinely on the reset-before-send path are reconciled to the corrected value (the pre-reset-Logon and peer-spontaneous pins, e.g. `BilateralStrict_Initiator_CountersResetToOne`, MUST stay green **unchanged**); the default (knob-off) path and the acceptor-role paths are byte-identical to the pre-fix baseline — verified by the existing test suite plus a byte-identity check on the affected admin frames. The blast radius includes any `by_peer_request` event-classification pins on the fixpp-initiated reset paths (reconciled per FR-006/SC-006), enumerated and individually justified during `/speckit-tasks`.
- **SC-006**: On the fixpp-initiated reset paths (`reset_on_logon`/`reset_on_logout`/`reset_on_disconnect`, any policy) the `sequence_numbers_reset` event carries `by_peer_request=false`; on the peer-initiated path (fixpp sent no `141=Y`) it carries `by_peer_request=true` — verified by an automated test; today the fixpp-initiated `reset_on_logon` + non-strict-policy path is mislabeled `true`.

## Normative References

- **[FIX-SL §4.4.2]** — *FIX Session-Layer*, `ResetSeqNumFlag(141)` semantics: a Logon carrying `141=Y` resets the sequence numbers to `1`; the Logon that carries the flag is itself sent/received at sequence `1` and thereby consumes it, so each side's next message after the reset is sequence `2`. A peer's confirming echo of a reset the other side initiated is an acknowledgement, not a second reset request.
- **Reference-engine oracle (grounding)** — QuickFIX-cpp v1.16.0 and QuickFIX-J 3.0.1 apply reset-then-increment: after a `141=Y` Logon exchange each side's next outbound is `2` because the Logon at seq `1` was counted. The exact post-reset outbound value for the peer-spontaneous (peer-initiated, fixpp-did-not-send-141) path is pinned with line citations in `research.md` / `plan.md` during planning.

## Assumptions

- The `reset_on_logon` knob and the `141=Y` wire semantics are otherwise as shipped in `024`; this feature changes only fixpp's outbound counter after the initiator honors a peer's `141=Y` **echo** of fixpp's own reset.
- The `030` inbound restore on this arm (next-inbound → `2`) and its fatal-when-persistent reset disposition are correct and are preserved; this feature adds the outbound twin without altering them.
- Reset-Logon **inbound**-advancement semantics (`141=Y` inbound) are owned by `030`; this feature is confined to the **outbound** counter and the `by_peer_request` event classification on the initiator `peer_ack_sent_reset_flag` arm.
- The reference engines (QuickFIX-cpp v1.16.0, QuickFIX-J 3.0.1) are the conformance oracle for the corrected outbound value, consistent with the project's per-release interop gate.
- The discriminating trigger (resolved in Clarifications) is **reset-before-send: fixpp emitted `141=Y` AND its own Logon went at the post-reset seq `1`**, covering all three reset knobs — not the policy-level `we_initiated`. The "fixpp emitted `141=Y`" half is captured as the literal emit-time fact (the `session.cpp:721` `initr_reset_seqnum` value), latched at emit and read on the ack arm — NOT reconstructed from config flags plus a counter sample (which drops the inbound-at-1 conjunct of the real emit predicate). The **mechanism is resolved at Gate A to restore-outbound-after-reset (Mechanism A, symmetric to `030`)**; the skip-the-redundant-reset alternative (Mechanism B) is **dropped** — it is unsound for the fresh `bilateral_strict`-at-`{1,1}` row (whose only durable reset on the path is the ack-arm reset, since the open-time reset gate `session.cpp:681` fires on `reset_on_logon` only). Mechanism A must satisfy FR-001/FR-006 **and** the FR-005 non-regression constraints.
- The fix's blast radius is to be determined empirically during `/speckit-tasks` / `/implement` by enumerating only the pins genuinely on the reset-before-send path (the `030`/`031` discipline) plus the `by_peer_request` event-classification pins reconciled per FR-006. In particular `BilateralStrict_Initiator_CountersResetToOne` in `test_reset_seqnum_policy_matrix.cpp` is **NOT** in the outbound-value blast radius: it pre-seeds outbound to `10` so its Logon is emitted at the **pre-reset** sequence `10`, which does not participate in the post-reset numbering, so its asserted `next_outbound==1` is **correct** and MUST NOT be flipped. Any pin to be changed must be verified to sit on the reset-before-send path first; pre-judging the set risks the `030` "symmetric restore" regression.
